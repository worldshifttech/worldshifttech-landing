## The One-Sentence Brand

**"I audit how businesses use AI, design the leanest solution that works, and direct the savings back into real planetary impact."**

Everything on the site, in proposals, in emails, and in social posts should be traceable back to this sentence.

---

## What Changed and Why

WST started as a custom software shop with a sustainability credential. That was honest but incomplete.

The sharper truth is this: most businesses in 2026 are using AI badly. Too many tools. Too many subscriptions. Too much compute running for too little result. They were sold a transformation and got a bloated stack instead. The planet pays part of that bill.

Drew's edge has always been precision — build only what needs to exist, run only what earns its keep. That's now the whole business, made explicit.

The pivot is not a rebrand. It's an honest sharpening. The voice, the colors, the founder presence — all the same. The mission is now visible.

## Brand Position

**Category:** AI Operations Auditor and Precision Builder for SMBs

**For:** Business owners, ops leads, and teams who adopted AI tools in the last two years and suspect they're paying for more than they're getting.

**Against:** Bloated AI stacks, vague sustainability claims, tools that promise everything and deliver noise.

**The promise:** I'll tell you what's wasted, design something leaner, build it, and put a portion of what you save into verified planetary programs. You get a better operation. The planet gets something real.

**The proof loop:**
1. Audit what you're running and what it costs — in dollars and compute
2. Design the minimal stack that actually does the job
3. Build the pieces that need to be built
4. Direct a fixed portion of project fees or client savings into vetted environmental programs — specific ones, named, not offsets

## Voice

**One sentence:** Plain-spoken, direct, slightly understated. The voice of someone who's been inside enough businesses to know what's broken and isn't impressed by hype.

### The Rules

**1. Say the thing directly.**
No warm-up. No "In today's world of AI..." Lead with the point.

- Yes: "Most AI stacks are 60% waste. I can show you which 60%."
- No: "As artificial intelligence continues to reshape the business landscape, organizations face new challenges..."

**2. Short sentences. Active verbs.**
If it runs past 20 words, break it. Read it aloud. If you wouldn't say it in a room, rewrite it.

**3. Name specific things.**
Tools, numbers, timelines, outcomes. Specificity is a trust signal. Vagueness is a red flag.

- Yes: "Your Make.com scenario is running 4,000 operations a month. 2,800 of them don't need to."
- No: "We optimize your automation workflows for maximum efficiency."

**4. No hype words. Ever.**
Banned: *revolutionize, transform, unlock, cutting-edge, next-generation, seamless, robust, game-changer, harness the power of, in the age of AI, paradigm shift, holistic, leverage* (as a verb).

These are now AI-generated copy tells. They also signal that whoever wrote them doesn't actually know what they're talking about.

**5. "You" more than "I" or "we."**
The reader is the protagonist. Drew is the operator behind the work.

- Yes: "You're probably running three tools that overlap. You know it. You just haven't had time to fix it."
- No: "WST delivers AI audit services that help clients optimize their technology investments."

**6. Be honest about tradeoffs.**
If a $30/month SaaS does what they need, say so. If the audit finds mostly good decisions, say that too. The willingness to say "you don't need a build" is the trust-earner.

**7. AI is a tool, not the story.**
Talk about AI the way you'd talk about a database or a spreadsheet. It's inside the work. It's not the headline.

- Yes: "The agent reads the email, drafts the reply, waits for your sign-off. That's it."
- No: "Harness the transformative power of agentic AI to revolutionize your inbox."

**8. No em-dashes.** Use commas, colons, or restructure.

**9. No reassurance language.** No "free consultation," "no pressure," "no obligation." State what happens plainly.

## The Three-Part Business Model (And How to Talk About Each)

### Part 1: The AI Audit

**What it is:** A structured review of a business's current AI and automation stack. What tools they're running, what they're paying, what compute those tools consume, what's actually working, and what's waste.

**What they get:** A plain-language report with:
- Current stack inventory with real cost breakdown
- Estimated compute and energy footprint
- What to cut, what to keep, what to replace
- A recommended minimal stack that does the same job

**How to talk about it:**

> Most businesses have been sold AI tools they don't fully use, running automations that fire on bad data, and paying subscriptions that overlap with things they already have. The audit is a 2-3 week diagnostic that tells you exactly what's wasted. No jargon. No upsell pressure. Just a clear picture.

**Price:** $500–$1,500 depending on stack size. Standalone purchase. No commitment to a build.

**The honest positioning note:** The audit sells itself because the problem is universal. Anyone who adopted AI tools in 2023 or 2024 has waste they haven't cleaned up yet. The audit is the entry point. It's also the proof that Drew knows what he's talking about before anyone hires him to build anything.

---

### Part 2: The Redesign and Build

**What it is:** After the audit, Drew designs the leaner replacement stack and builds whatever custom pieces are needed. Same build service as before — integrations, custom apps, AI agents — now with a clear mission behind it.

**What they get:** The minimal viable technical solution. Built once. Owned outright. No subscription to Drew.

**How to talk about it:**

> The build comes after the audit. Sometimes that means a custom integration connecting two tools that should have been talking for years. Sometimes it's a single AI agent replacing three separate subscriptions. Sometimes it's nothing — the stack just needs to be trimmed, not rebuilt. The outcome is the leanest version of your operation that actually works.

**Price:** $2,000–$10,500 fixed scope, fixed price. Same as before.

---

### Part 3: The Redirect

**What it is:** A portion of every project fee goes into verified environmental programs. Not carbon offsets. Actual programs: reforestation, ocean cleanup, renewable energy infrastructure in underserved areas. Drew chooses programs based on transparency, accountability, and avoidance of companies with contradictory environmental records.

**What they get:** A line item on every invoice showing where their portion goes. A link to the program. Real proof.

**How to talk about it:**

> AI has an energy cost. Every tool running, every model inference, every automation firing — it adds up. I'm not going to pretend otherwise. What I can do is design solutions that use as little as they need to, and direct a fixed portion of every project into programs that are actually doing something about the larger problem. It's not a fix. It's a commitment to doing the work honestly.

**The precision required here:** Never claim "carbon neutral" or "net zero" without third-party certification. Never say "for every project we plant a tree" — it's become meaningless. Name the specific programs. Name the amounts. Be specific or say nothing.

**Avoid platforms with documented greenwashing records.** Research before committing to any program partner.

## The Green Positioning Rules (Non-Negotiable)

These rules exist because greenwashing destroys trust faster than almost anything else. Be specific or say nothing.

**Say:**
- "A fixed portion of every project goes into [specific program name]"
- "Verified by [third-party organization]"
- "Lean code by default — no idle infrastructure, no bloat"
- "Hosted on infrastructure committed to renewable energy" (accurate for Vercel/AWS)

**Never say:**
- "Carbon neutral"
- "Net zero"
- "We plant a tree for every project"
- "Eco-friendly software"
- "Green technology"
- "Sustainable AI"
- "Running on 100% renewable energy" (not accurate for Vercel/AWS)

**Before naming any program partner:** research their accountability record, their reporting transparency, and whether they have any documented ties to companies with contradictory environmental practices.
